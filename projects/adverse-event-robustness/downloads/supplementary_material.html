READUS-PV checklist

The numbered READUS-PV items are mapped to the manuscript below. Partial coverage and assessments not performed are stated explicitly.

READUS-PV itemWhere addressed or applicability
1a–bTitle. The title identifies disproportionality, the FDA quarterly files, both drug classes and the study period; the five reactions are listed in the Abstract.
2a–cRationale. Sections 1 and 4.2 explain the processing problem and prior evidence. Section 2.5 gives the fixed event definitions and their labeling sources. This is a methods study, not an estimate of event frequency in treated patients.
3Objective. Abstract Objective and the final paragraph of Section 1; the database-wide background is defined in Section 2.6.
4a–bDesign. Sections 2.1–2.7 describe the factorial disproportionality analysis, the primary configuration and four secondary analyses. The plan and amendments are in ESM 1.
5aData and terminology. Sections 2.2, 2.4 and 2.5 identify FDA, the quarterly files, RxNorm/RxClass and the exact MedDRA preferred-term strings. ESM 2 Tables S1 and S3 document release compatibility and terms. Section 4.4 explains the lack of a fixed MedDRA hierarchy/version across quarters.
5bPreprocessing. Sections 2.2–2.4, Table 1 and Fig. 1 describe retrieval, exclusions, case versions, deletion lists and matching. Source dates and checksums are in ESM 3.
6a–dVariables. Sections 2.2–2.7 define the report population, exposure, events, roles, matching and comparison groups, and explain how frozen RxNav responses inform drug identification. ESM 2 Tables S3–S5 give the exact terms and names.
7aDescriptive analyses. Section 2.7 identifies the post hoc profiles of moved units; Section 3.4 and ESM 2 Table S7 report drug/reaction counts and reporter characteristics. No demographic subgroup or hypothesis test was added.
7bMeasure and thresholds. Section 2.6 defines the ROR, Woolf 95% interval and minimum-cell rules. Section 2.7 compares estimates and interval limits with 1 descriptively; no new safety-signal discovery threshold is claimed.
7cSensitivity analyses. Section 2.7, Tables 2–4 and Sections 3.3–3.5. The active-comparator analysis changes the background; no confounder-adjusted causal estimate is reported.
7d–eOther analyses. No clinical case-by-case causality assessment was performed. The author’s raw-record and mapping checks in Section 2.9 verify processing. RxNav supplies names and class membership, not a separate statistical sample.
8aReport flow. Section 3.1, Table 1 and Fig. 1 give the report/version/case counts and exclusions.
8bCase characteristics. Section 3.4 and ESM 2 Table S7 describe moved report groups and reporter characteristics. Age, sex and detailed clinical profiles were not analyzed; this reporting item is only partly addressed.
9Estimates. Tables 2 and 4 report every principal and secondary estimate with its four cells and interval; Fig. 2 shows the principal configurations. Table 3 summarizes case-level robustness.
10Clinical case assessment. Not performed. No patient-level causal adjudication or new clinical safety-signal assessment is claimed; see Sections 2.9 and 4.4.
11Interpretation. Sections 4.1–4.2 distinguish processing sensitivity in fixed, previously communicated pairs from discovery. The arthralgia analysis uses a broader term than severe, disabling joint pain in the label.
12a–cApplicability. Sections 4.3–4.4 and 5 limit the conclusions to report processing and five pairs. They do not establish patient risk or treatment recommendations. Extension to other windows and event definitions requires further study.
13Limitations. Section 4.4 describes reporting and indication biases, incomplete case histories, unresolved duplicates, term coverage and the lack of causal or incidence interpretation.
14a–cDeclarations. Funding, Conflicts of Interest, Ethics approval and consent statements identify internal resources, ownership of Norlowe Research and the use of public de-identified data.
