The question

An analyst checking a systematic review’s reference list for retractions, corrections and expressions of concern needs to know three things. How much of the list can be checked? How much does the result depend on the status source? How often can a notice be dated before or after the review appeared? This report answers these questions for a fixed cohort of 1,000 open-access systematic reviews and meta-analyses on diabetes, obesity and heart failure, using a frozen Crossref Retraction Watch snapshot (data for 2026-10-06) and PubMed records retrieved on 2026-10-07.

Summary. Of 62,740 reference occurrences, 92.2% carried a usable identifier and 88.9% resolved to one PubMed record. Exact DOI matching found every unambiguous Retraction Watch link that a verified identifier crosswalk found. Adding PubMed relations changed which studies were flagged. PubMed alone recorded 7 retraction-flagged and 10 concern-flagged occurrences, Retraction Watch alone recorded 4 and 4, and nearly all corrections appeared only in PubMed. Of 46 retraction-flagged occurrences, 16 had a notice dated before the review went online, 28 after, and 2 could not be placed.

The audit works with metadata. It does not judge any study or review, and it does not show that any review’s conclusions changed.

Data

  • Frame. One PubMed query was run on 2026-10-07. It covered diabetes, obesity or heart failure (MeSH or title), the publication types Systematic Review or Meta-Analysis, publication dates from 2015 to 2025, and PubMed’s “pubmed pmc open access” filter. The 7,993 PMIDs were frozen with a SHA-256 hash before any status lookup.
  • Selection. The first 1,000 PMIDs were taken in order of the SHA-256 hash of a fixed salt plus the PMID, without replacement.
  • Full text. JATS XML came from the PMC Article Datasets on AWS, the route PMC lists for automated retrieval [1, 2], and each file was checked against its published MD5 digest.
  • Status sources. The Crossref Retraction Watch CSV at commit fe345427 [3], and PubMed CommentsCorrections relations plus the Retracted Publication type, retrieved through the E-utilities at no more than 2 requests per second [4].

Methods

Units. A reference occurrence is one citation in one review’s reference list. There are 62,740, a median of 55 per review. Unique cited works (48,691), reviews and notices are reported separately and never added together.

Linkage arms.

Arm Identifiers used Status source
A DOIs written in the reference Retraction Watch
B DOI, PMID and PMCID in the reference, plus NCBI-verified crosswalks (PMC ID Converter, the DOI on the PubMed record, and a DOI search accepted only on exact DOI equality) Retraction Watch
C As B Retraction Watch and PubMed relations

Ambiguity and notices. References whose identifiers point to different PubMed records, or whose DOI disagrees with the resolved record, are labeled ambiguous and sent to manual review. A reference that cites a notice (its DOI or PMID is a notice identifier) is never treated as citing the retracted article.

Chronology. Dates are intervals at their real precision. A notice is “before review” only if its interval ends before the review’s first online date interval begins, “after review” only if it begins after that interval ends, and indeterminate otherwise.

Statistics. Occurrence-level proportions use 2,000 review-level cluster bootstrap resamples (seed 20261007) [5]. Shares among flagged occurrences use Wilson intervals. The plan fixed five sensitivity analyses in advance, and three later amendments are dated in docs/analysis_plan.md.

Results

What could be checked

Every cited PMID resolved to a PubMed record and no request failed after retries. Of the 7.8% without identifiers, 40.9% were tagged as journal articles and 24.2% as books; the rest were web pages, reports and other material. Restricting to tagged identifiers (S1) changed coverage by 0.11 percentage points.

What each arm flagged

Arm B added 2 occurrences to arm A, both corrections in references with conflicting identifiers. One cited the article under its Crossref-registered DOI, while Retraction Watch and PubMed list an alias DOI that Crossref redirects to it. The other paired the article’s PMID with its correction notice’s DOI. No unambiguous link came from a crosswalk.

When the notice appeared

Most indeterminate correction timing came from 783 PubMed erratum relations that name the notice only by a citation string. In the post hoc, exploratory amendment A1, every one of those strings carried a date, and all placed the erratum before the review.

One real case: two sources, two dates

A review of obesity and COVID-19 mortality (PMC7901910) first appeared online on 5 February 2021. Its reference 41 cites an earlier meta-analysis (PMID 32660813, DOI 10.1016/j.orcp.2020.07.002). Both sources record a retraction of that article. Retraction Watch (record 25648) dates the notice 6 February 2021, one day after the review appeared. PubMed’s notice record (PMID 33563391) has no electronic date and is dated by its January 2021 issue, which falls before the review. The audit keeps both claims and calls the timing indeterminate. The review’s two sentences that cite the article compare findings and describe the cited meta-analysis, and neither mentions a retraction. Settling the order would need the publisher’s own notice date, which is why cases like this go to the review queue.

Sensitivity analyses

References that cite a notice, and the title queue

In 50 occurrence and notice pairs (45 reference occurrences), the reference cited the notice itself (43 published errata and 3 retraction notices in PubMed, and 3 retraction notices and 1 correction notice in Retraction Watch). The prespecified title matcher produced 0 candidates among references without an identifier link.

Validation

Five kinds of checks were run and are reported separately.

  • Software tests. 55 tests passed on synthetic fixtures covering DOI punctuation and prefixes, duplicated notices, notice DOIs cited in place of the article, conflicting PMIDs, incomplete dates and failed requests. Fixtures use the test DOI prefix 10.5555 and never enter study tables.

  • Source reconciliation. A separate routine re-read every arm C claim from the raw Retraction Watch CSV (Python csv) and the raw cached PubMed XML (xml.etree): 2,522 of 2,522 confirmed. Count reconciliation across stages and MD5 checks of all review XML passed. SQL timing matched a separate Python implementation on every claim.

  • Re-check against fresh records. The 120 flagged claim rows were checked again against PubMed records retrieved anew and Crossref’s metadata for each cited DOI: 64 of 64 PubMed claims present, notice dates unchanged for 63 of 63, and the record listed by Crossref with the same type and date for 54 of 56 Retraction Watch claims (Crossref does not list one correction and one reinstatement record). The 100 sampled references gave the same identifiers under a separate regular-expression extraction (100 of 100), and every parsed identifier appeared in PMC’s text of the review (100 of 100).

  • Determinism. Build and analysis were rerun offline from cached inputs: all 36 output tables identical.

  • Manual verification. I checked every retraction, expression of concern and reinstatement claim, every link first made in arm B, and a stratified random sample of 100 reference occurrences against the Retraction Watch record, the PubMed records, the publisher notice and the PMC article page, and found them consistent with the source records.

These checks test the software and its agreement with the sources. They do not validate the sources themselves.

Limitations

The cohort is a deterministic sample of one indexing-defined frame of PMC open-access reviews and is not a census. Coverage describes agreement with two sources at their snapshot dates, not sensitivity to all real retractions, and a reference with no recorded claim is not shown to be sound. Retraction Watch and PubMed share upstream notices, so their agreement is not independent confirmation. Flagged counts are small. Citation does not show that a review used a study’s data, and no pooled effect was re-analyzed. Citation sentences are shown only for Creative Commons reviews, and only explicit citation links were used.

Reproduce

python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -e ".[test,report]"
.venv/bin/rae acquire     # network, about 15 minutes, cached
.venv/bin/rae build       # linkage, ledger, claims, timing
.venv/bin/rae analyze     # tables, figures, workbooks
.venv/bin/rae validate    # tests, reconciliation, offline rerun
.venv/bin/rae render      # this report and the manuscript

rae audit my_bibliography.csv --review-date 2025-06-01 runs the same linkage on any CSV or RIS bibliography and writes a review queue workbook.
