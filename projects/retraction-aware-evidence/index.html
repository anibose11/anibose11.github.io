Retracted, replaced or corrected: what a reference check of 1,000 systematic reviews finds

Animesh Bose, Norlowe Research

An analyst checking a systematic review's references for retractions, corrections and expressions of concern needs to know how much of the list can be checked, how much the result depends on the status source, and whether each notice came before or after the review. This study measured all three in 1,000 open-access reviews of diabetes, obesity and heart failure, covering 62,740 reference occurrences.

Corrected October 2026. An item-level check by AI agents (Claude Opus 5.5 via Claude Code) on 24 October 2026 found two references wrongly linked to retracted studies. Both were removed under post hoc amendment A4, and the counts on this page are the corrected ones; the version of 7 October 2026 reported 46 retraction-flagged occurrences in 41 reviews.

  • 92.2%of reference occurrences carried a usable DOI, PMID or PMCID, and 7.8% had none
  • 7 in 7 reviewsreferences citing a study retracted, for a reason other than replacement, at least six months before the review appeared (post hoc amendment A5)
  • 44 in 40 reviewsreference occurrences citing 42 works with a retraction claim. PubMed alone recorded 7 occurrences and Retraction Watch alone 3

The audit works with metadata. A flag means that a status source records a notice for the cited work as of the snapshot date. It does not judge the study, the review or anyone's conduct, and citing a study after its notice does not show that the review relied on it.

Where the data come from

The review frame is one PubMed query run on 7 October 2026. It covers systematic reviews and meta-analyses on diabetes, obesity or heart failure published from 2015 to 2025 that are in the PMC Open Access Subset (7,993 records). The first 1,000 in order of a hash of their PMIDs were taken, without replacement, and their JATS XML was retrieved from the PMC Article Datasets on AWS. Status claims come from the Crossref Retraction Watch CSV pinned to one commit and from PubMed's own correction and retraction relations. Every response is cached with its SHA-256 hash, so the whole analysis reruns offline.

How the audit links a reference to a status notice

  1. Parse referencesOne row per citation in the review's JATS reference list, with every DOI, PMID and PMCID and where each one came from.
  2. Arm AExact DOI matching against Retraction Watch.
  3. Arm BDOI, PMID, PMCID and NCBI-verified crosswalks against Retraction Watch. Conflicting identifiers go to manual review.
  4. Arm CAdds PubMed retraction, correction and concern relations, each kept as its own claim with its own date.
  5. Dates as intervalsA notice counts as before or after the review only when the date intervals do not overlap.
  6. Review queueA spreadsheet with identifiers, claims, dates, timing, match confidence and source links.

How the audit handles disagreements

Each source keeps its own claim and date

A review of obesity and COVID-19 mortality (PMC7901910) first appeared online on 5 February 2021 and cites a meta-analysis that both sources record as retracted. Retraction Watch dates the notice 6 February 2021, a day after the review. PubMed's notice record carries only an issue date of January–February 2021, an interval that overlaps the review's date. A single "cited after retraction" label would hide that uncertainty, so the audit keeps both claims and marks the timing as indeterminate. Of 44 retraction-flagged occurrences, 2 were indeterminate.

Conflicting identifiers go to manual review

The identifier crosswalk added only 1 link, a correction in a reference whose identifiers disagreed: it used the article's Crossref-registered DOI, while Retraction Watch and PubMed list an alias that Crossref redirects to it. The audit labeled it ambiguous and listed it for manual review. (A second link reported in the 7 October version came from a reference that cites the erratum itself; amendment A4 now treats it as a cited notice.) In total, 321 reference occurrences (0.5%) were labeled ambiguous; in a sample of 20 of them checked by AI agents, 8 were one work cited under variant DOIs.

What the results do not show

The cohort is a deterministic sample of PMC open-access reviews selected through PubMed indexing and is not a census. A match means agreement with two sources at their snapshot dates, which is different from sensitivity to every real retraction. Retraction Watch and PubMed also share upstream notices, so their agreement is not independent confirmation. The flagged counts are small, and a post hoc replication in the frame's other 6,993 records (amendment A7) found more retraction and concern flags per reference (0.104% against 0.070% for retractions), so the retraction and concern counts on this page are probably low. A citation does not show that a review used the study's data, and no pooled result was re-analyzed.

How the results were checked

The test suite passes on synthetic fixtures covering DOI punctuation and prefixes, duplicated notices, notice DOIs cited in place of the article, conflicting PMIDs, incomplete dates and failed requests (55 tests in the version of 7 October 2026; 81 after the October corrections). After the correction, a separate routine in the same code base confirmed 2,514 of 2,514 status claims against the raw source records, and an offline rerun reproduced every output file. A worksheet for a manual check of the flagged claims and a 100-reference sample was prepared on 7 October 2026, but no result was recorded in it. On 24 October 2026, AI agents (Claude Opus 5.5 via Claude Code) checked all 120 flagged claim rows and the 100 sampled references against the source records, with two verifiers using different methods and a third reconciling them. They found two references wrongly linked to retracted studies, one of them a 2014 conference abstract cited by the DOI of its whole abstract supplement, which Retraction Watch lists for a different, retracted abstract. Both were removed under post hoc amendment A4, and the counts on this page exclude them.

Reproduce the analysis

python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -e ".[test,report]"
.venv/bin/rae demo          # fixtures only, seconds
.venv/bin/rae acquire       # network, about 15 minutes, cached
.venv/bin/rae build && .venv/bin/rae analyze && .venv/bin/rae validate
.venv/bin/rae audit my_refs.csv --review-date 2025-06-01
